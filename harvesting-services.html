Standard Operating Procedure

Field Operations & Post-Harvest SOP

4 synchronized checkpoints ensuring export-spec fruit never touches bare ground from stalk severance to refrigerated docking.

01

Field De-Handing & Precision Sorting

Our crews execute surgical crown cuts using sterilized curved blades. This avoids tearing vascular conduits, leaving a clean, firm peduncle base that prevents crown rot during reefer ocean shipment. Hands are segregated by finger count (14–18 fingers per hand) directly under shaded field pergolas.

verified Surgical crown resection without pulp bruising
02

Latex Washing & De-Gumming Baths

Immediately post-severing, banana hands enter mobile food-grade tanks containing circulating fresh water and mild citric neutralizer. This immediately stops sticky latex dripping that would otherwise stain peel surfaces black, ensuring flawless export-grade visual appeal upon arrival.

water Double-rinse sap neutralizing system
03

Foam-Cushioned Field Crating

Fruits are arranged inside heavy-duty perforated crates layered with 6mm polyethylene foam dividers. Finger tips never scrape adjacent crown wood, eliminating scuffing, vibration scarring, and friction tears during farm-to-packhouse transport over unpaved field trails.

inventory_2 Shock-absorbent high-density foam layering
04

Synchronized Cold-Transit Docking

From field severing to sealed refrigerated cargo, our logistical window is locked at < 4 hours. Field temperature pulp (28–32°C) is transferred into pre-cooled insulated reefer containers (13.5°C set point) to immediately arrest ethylene production and slow respiration rate.
